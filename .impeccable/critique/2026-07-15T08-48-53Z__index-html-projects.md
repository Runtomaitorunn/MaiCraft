## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This does not immediately look AI-generated. The cold-paper palette, restrained corners, and varied real project media feel authored. The section is still a borderline pass because it falls into familiar portfolio-template grammar: equal two-column cards, small uppercase metadata, oversized ghost type, uppercase titles, and identical hover behavior. The brand promises fresh, bold, and technical, but the composition makes no bold curatorial decision. "Selected Work" treats eight projects as interchangeable inventory.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json index.html` reported three warnings in `index.html`: `overused-font` at line 13 for Montserrat, `single-font` at line 13, and `broken-image` at line 99 for the dialog poster's initial empty `src`. The `single-font` result is a false positive because `styles.css` defines separate display and body stacks. The empty dialog image is dynamically populated before opening, so it is not a normal broken-image state, although omitting `src` initially would be cleaner. The Montserrat warning is context-sensitive: it is intended as a fallback, but it may become the effective display face if the local Neue font files are unavailable. The markup-only detector could not inspect the project cards generated by `script.js`.

**Visual overlays:** Browser automation exposed no browser backend, so no fresh tab, injection preflight, live overlay, or console evidence was available. No user-visible overlay exists. ## Overall Impression

The section demonstrates real range, but it asks visitors to curate the portfolio themselves. Its biggest opportunity is to turn a flat inventory into a directed argument: one signature project, a small set of supporting proofs, and a visibly lighter experimental archive.

The emotional journey starts with anticipation, peaks when strong Pagoda, Aldar, Qianfan, and Safety Pit media appears, then drops when placeholder-led or inert cards read as unfinished. The end broadens Ye's personality, but it does not land a memorable claim about Ye's highest-value capability.

Cognitive load is moderate with 2 of 8 checklist failures: visual hierarchy and minimal choices. The XR group presents five equal-priority options, exceeding the four-item working-memory threshold without a featured path. Chunking, category grouping, and the one-column mobile layout otherwise work well.

## What's Working

2. **XR and Playground is a useful strategic split.** It separates professional and research proof from experimentation without hiding either side of Ye's identity.
3. **The interaction foundation is thoughtful.** Semantic links, buttons and articles, meaningful alt text, visible focus styles, a native dialog, reduced-motion support, and large card targets provide a solid accessibility baseline.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] "Selected Work" has no selection hierarchy

**Why it matters:** Recruiters, collaborators, and mentors scan for a signature project and strongest evidence within seconds. Five equal XR cards make them do the curatorial work.

**Fix:** Feature one project, likely *Midnight at the Pagoda*, across the full width or in a 2:1 composition. Give it one explicit outcome and one technical signal. Rank two commercial or research projects as secondary, reduce the remaining XR work to compact editorial rows, and give Playground a visibly lighter rhythm.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] Interaction outcomes are visually indistinguishable

**Why it matters:** An internal case study, external website, video dialog, and unavailable article look like the same action. This breaks learned expectations and makes inert projects feel broken.

**Fix:** Add persistent affordances such as "Read case study," "Watch project film," "Visit live project ↗," and "Archive note." Keep video marks visible. Remove actionable hover treatment from inert cards or give every item a meaningful destination.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Proof disappears where the brand needs it most

**Why it matters:** The supplied DOM shows placeholder thumbnails for BeatBoxVR and Habibi even though local poster assets exist, while Visual Grammar has no image. Placeholder-led cards contradict the media-first design principle and create an unfinished midpoint.

**Fix:** Verify that `poster` is reliably rendered for YouTube projects, fix any runtime or stale-content mismatch, and supply one decisive frame for Visual Grammar. Give the GAVIS video a poster or still fallback so slow, data-saving, and reduced-motion contexts still receive visual proof.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Project copy describes topics instead of value

**Why it matters:** "VR heritage and memory," "Classroom VR onboarding," and "XR hackathon prototype" do not tell an evaluator what Ye made, owned, or achieved. The award is buried in metadata, and the Aldar sentence is truncated by single-line CSS.

**Fix:** Standardize cards around `Year · Role · Context`, followed by one concise sentence combining contribution and result. Normalize capitalization such as "App development." Allow two balanced lines or shorten every summary to a tested length.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time visitor:** HCI, R&D, XR, and virtual twins are untranslated. Identical cards do not say whether a click opens a case study, another website, or a video. Placeholder media looks broken, and terse summaries do not answer what Ye actually did.

**Riley, stress tester:** A failed `content.json` request can leave the generated section empty without recovery. Similar cards produce four outcomes: internal navigation, new tab, modal, or no action. Runtime DOM and available poster assets appear out of sync for BeatBoxVR and Habibi.

**Casey, distracted mobile visitor:** The one-column layout is readable, but eight full-size cards produce a long, undifferentiated scroll. Autoplay video and animated GIF assets add media cost. One-line ellipsis hides commercial value, and action types are not labeled before tapping.

**Morgan, recruiter or research collaborator:** Breadth is visible within 20 to 30 seconds, but Ye's strongest capability is not. Roles, ownership, deliverables, and outcomes are inconsistent. Award-winning work receives no stronger status than incomplete or noninteractive items.

## Minor Observations

- The small `h2` "Selected Work" followed by much larger `h3` group titles creates a semantic and visual hierarchy mismatch.
- "Projects," "Selected Work," and "XR Projects" repeat the same section concept before the first proof appears.
- Uppercasing every project title suppresses the identity of individual project names.
- `white-space: nowrap` makes summary quality depend on viewport width.
- External links open new tabs without a persistent external-destination cue.
- `single-font` is a detector false positive, but relying on an unbundled Neue stack can still make Montserrat the real display face.
- The `src=""` dialog poster warning is mostly false positive because JavaScript assigns the source before opening; omitting the attribute initially would reduce ambiguity.
